# Knot & Bloom: Vision & Strategy

This document serves as our North Star. It outlines the core goal, vision, and the specific problems Knot & Bloom exists to solve. Whenever we make product, design, or business decisions, they should be measured against these principles.

---

## Our Goal

To become the most **trusted and curated** marketplace for handcrafted goods in the Philippines — built *specifically* for students, single parents, hobbyists, and small handcrafters who deserve a real selling platform without the complexity of big e-commerce or the chaos of social media.

---

## Who We Are Building For

Knot & Bloom is built for a very specific, underserved group of Filipino micro-creators:

- 🎓 **Students** selling handmade crafts on the side to support themselves
- 👩‍👧 **Single parents** turning a skill or hobby into extra income from home
- 🧶 **Hobbyists** who want to monetize their passion without running a full business
- 🪡 **Small handcrafters** producing limited quantities of genuine, handmade goods

These creators share one thing in common: they are **individual or micro-scale operators** — not businesses with dedicated staff, logistics teams, or marketing budgets. Every hour they spend managing DMs, verifying payments, or setting up a storefront is an hour taken away from the craft itself.

---

## Our Vision

We envision a digital ecosystem where local craftsmanship is protected and celebrated. By providing a low-friction, trusted, and curated platform, we empower micro-creators to sell sustainably and confidently — without the overwhelming setup of enterprise e-commerce tools or the instability of social media selling. Buyers, in turn, get absolute confidence that every item is authentic, handcrafted, and purchased from a verified Filipino maker.

---

## The Gap We Fill

### Why Big Platforms Don't Work for Micro-Creators

Platforms like Shopee and Lazada require business registrations, official documents, and commercial-scale inventory management. For a student crocheting plushies between classes or a single parent making resin jewelry after bedtime, these requirements are a wall — not a door. Even when listings go live, handmade items are invisible, buried under thousands of factory-made imports with lower prices and inflated review counts.

### Why Social Media Isn't Enough

Facebook groups, Instagram shops, and TikTok lives have become the *de facto* selling channels for micro-creators in the Philippines — but they were never designed for commerce. Running a business through social media means:

- Manually responding to hundreds of DMs to confirm orders
- Screenshot-chasing GCash payments and cross-referencing them manually
- No order tracking, no dispute resolution, no buyer protection
- Building trust from scratch with every single customer
- Spending more time on the "business" than on the actual craft

For a solo creator, this is an unsustainable burden that burns people out before their business ever has a chance to grow.

---

## The Problems We Are Solving

### 1. The Access Problem (Big Platforms Are Not Built for Them)
- **For Micro-Creators:** Selling on Shopee or Lazada requires documentation, processes, and scale that individual hobbyists and students simply don't have.
- **Our Solution:** Knot & Bloom has a seller onboarding flow designed for individuals — no business registration required to start, just a verified identity and genuine handcrafted goods. We are the platform that says *yes* when the big ones say *not yet*.

### 2. The Curation Problem (The "Sea of Mass Production")
- **For Buyers:** Finding genuine handcrafted items on major marketplaces is nearly impossible; they are buried under thousands of cheap, factory-made imports.
- **Our Solution:** Strict product and seller curation. Every artisan and item is vetted, ensuring that when a buyer comes to Knot & Bloom, 100% of the catalog is authentic and handcrafted. We are a sanctuary from mass production.

### 3. The Trust Deficit in Social Commerce
- **For Buyers:** Buying from Instagram or Facebook requires a massive leap of faith — sending money to a stranger's bank account and hoping the item ships. Scams are rampant.
- **Our Solution:** We act as the trusted intermediary. We handle secure payments, offer order tracking, and manage dispute resolutions, making buying from a local student or hobbyist as safe as buying from a major retailer.

### 4. The Financial Risk of COD (Cash on Delivery)
- **For Sellers:** COD is the lifeblood of Philippine e-commerce, but "bogus buyers" (people who refuse the package) force small artisans to absorb the cost of materials and return shipping — a fatal blow to micro-businesses with razor-thin margins.
- **Our Solution:** Our **20% COD Deposit** and **Customer Trust Score** system. We weed out bad actors before the item is even made, ensuring sellers only spend their time and materials on committed buyers.

### 5. The Operational Burden for Solo Creators
- **For Sellers:** Managing DMs, manually checking GCash receipts, tracking orders in spreadsheets, and handling customer inquiries takes time away from the actual craft — and for a single person, that time is finite.
- **Our Solution:** We provide an automated, trusted infrastructure — from AI-assisted product listings to unified order management and tracking — so micro-creators can stop being full-time customer service reps and focus on being artisans.

---

## Our Principle

> Every feature we build must answer one question: *Does this make it meaningfully easier for a Filipino student, single parent, hobbyist, or small hancrafter to sell what they make?* If the answer is no, we don't build it.
