## 1.  Vision & Guiding Principle

To become the most trusted and curated marketplace for handcrafted goods in the Philippines — built specifically for students, single parents, hobbyists, and small handcrafters who deserve a real selling platform without the complexity of big e-commerce or the chaos of social media.

### Who We Build For

- Students selling handmade crafts on the side to support themselves
- Single parents turning a skill or hobby into extra income from home
- Hobbyists who want to monetize their passion without running a full business
- Small handcrafters producing limited quantities of genuine, handmade goods

### Guiding Principle

> Every feature must answer one question: *"Does this make it meaningfully easier for a Filipino student, single parent, hobbyist, or small handcrafter to sell what they make?"* If the answer is no, we don't build it.

## 3.  Customer Segments

### Sellers (Supply Side)

Individual and micro-scale Filipino creators — not registered businesses. Defined by producing genuine, limited-quantity handcrafted goods, and by lacking the staff, logistics, or marketing budget of a commercial seller.

### Buyers (Demand Side)

Shoppers seeking authentic, verifiably handmade goods, who are currently underserved by both mass-market marketplaces (Shopee, Lazada) and unstructured social commerce (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok).

---

## 4.  Value Propositions

### For Sellers

- No business registration required — verified identity and genuine handcrafted goods are enough to start
- Protection from bogus COD buyers via the 20% deposit and Trust Score system
- Reduced operational burden — unified order management in place of DMs, spreadsheets, and manual GCash tracking

### For Buyers

- A fully curated catalog — 100% authentic, handcrafted, vetted sellers
- Secure payments and order tracking in place of a stranger's bank account
- Confidence they are supporting a verified Filipino maker

## 5.  Revenue Streams

### Stream 1 — Transaction Commission (Split Fee)

A percentage of each completed sale. To keep the burden low on creators, the 12% platform revenue is split:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Seller Side (5%)** | A flat 5% transaction fee deducted from the final sale value. |
| **Buyer Side (7%)** | A 7% "Platform & Trust Fee" added at checkout to fund secure escrow and 24/7 dispute resolution. |
| **Applied to** | The product subtotal. The COD deposit is a pure pass-through and is never commissioned directly. |

### Stream 2 — Seller Subscriptions (Phase 2)

Optional paid tiers that lower a seller's commission rate and add visibility and analytics benefits. Deferred at launch — activated only once there is enough seller volume for the tiers to mean anything (see Section 12).

| Tier | Details |
|---|---|
| **Free** | ₱0/mo · baseline commission · basic analytics |
| **Growth** | ₱149/mo · reduced commission · minor discovery boost · actionable analytics |
| **Studio** | ₱249/mo · lowest commission · higher discovery boost · full analytics · fastest payout release |

### Stream 3 — Promotion / Boost

A single, low-commitment option at launch: ₱49 for a 3-day homepage/category boost. Intentionally simple — an easy impulse purchase rather than a marketing decision.

## 6.  Channels

### Seller Acquisition

- Founder's personal crafter network — first, highest-trust cohort
- Facebook crafter/hobbyist groups — highest-yield channel for volume
- Instagram / TikTok — creators already posting handmade process content
- School and university craft communities — direct match to the student segment

### Buyer Acquisition

- Facebook and Instagram/TikTok, weighted equally — different buyer psychology, both relevant
- Sellers themselves as a distribution channel — each onboarded seller shares their storefront with their own existing audience

> **Note:** Channels function as trust chains, not ad placements: the founder's personal trust transfers to first sellers, whose success stories transfer trust into crafter communities, which transfers to their buyers. This mirrors the platform's core value proposition — trust — and should stay explicit as channels are executed, not just treated as a media-buying decision.

## 10.  Key Partnerships

| Partner | Role |
|---|---|
| **PayMongo** | Core dependency — every commission, deposit, and payout runs through it. Actively monitored, not treated as a plug-and-play vendor. |
| **Couriers (e.g. J&T)** | Informal — sellers self-manage shipping and courier choice. No exclusive contract or integration required at launch. |
| **DTI / BIR** | Required administrative relationship for Knot & Bloom's legal operation as a business entity. |
| **Crafter communities & school orgs** | Informal, relationship-based — doubles as the seller acquisition pipeline (see Section 6). |

The list is intentionally short. Because sellers self-handle logistics, the platform's real dependencies are payments (PayMongo) and people (community relationships) — not a web of vendor contracts. This suits a solo, self-funded founder.

## 12.  Phased Rollout Strategy

### Phase 1 — Launch

- Free tier only — 5% seller commission, 7% buyer fee, no subscription complexity
- Single promotion option — ₱49 / 3-day boost
- Founder handles all operations personally; self-service support model

### Phase 2 — Trigger to Unlock Growth / Studio Tiers

**Trigger:** 100 active sellers AND an average of 2+ sales per seller per month — both conditions required, not either alone. This avoids activating paid tiers on headcount that isn't actually transacting.

- Introduce tiered commission (Free / Growth / Studio) once real transaction data replaces launch assumptions
- Revisit promotion packages and seller-community moderation needs

## 13.  Transaction Flow & Core Policies

Verified end-to-end using a worked example: a ₱850 custom order, from a buyer discovering a seller's storefront through to final payout.

### 13.1  How Money Moves

- Buyer pays a 20% deposit through the payment partner (e.g. ₱170 on a ₱850 order); funds are held in escrow, not by Knot & Bloom directly
- Remaining 80% is paid as true cash-on-delivery, directly between buyer and seller — this portion never touches the platform
- Buyer pays a 7% Platform & Trust fee at checkout in addition to the order subtotal.
- On order completion, the 5% seller commission is deducted, and the remainder is released to the seller.

> **Note:** This mechanism only works securely because the deposit rate (20%) is set higher than the seller commission rate (5%). This is a hard constraint: seller commission may never be set higher than the prevailing deposit rate, or there is no held balance to deduct it from.

### 13.2  Cancellation & Refund Policy

- Deposit is non-refundable if the buyer cancels — protects the seller, who may have already purchased materials
- Deposit is refundable if the seller fails to fulfill the order — protects the buyer from a non-performing seller

### 13.3  Buyer Protection Policy

- The 20% deposit is the platform's only real escrow lever, and the only funds it can act on directly
- The 80% cash-COD portion is peer-to-peer once it changes hands; Knot & Bloom does not mediate or hold these funds
- Disputes on the cash portion are resolved directly between buyer and seller; the platform's enforcement is reputational — the Trust Score — not a refund from held funds

> **Note:** This is a genuine, deliberate limit on buyer protection — not an oversight. It should be stated plainly to buyers at checkout so expectations match what the platform can structurally deliver, rather than left implied.

## 14.  Key Risks & Open Considerations

| Risk | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founder as single point of failure** | Curation, dispute resolution, and platform maintenance currently depend entirely on one person's availability. |
| **Commission vs. deposit ceiling** | Any future commission rate must stay below the deposit rate (20%), or the payout mechanism breaks. |
| **Buyer-protection promise vs. reality** | "As safe as a major retailer" holds for the deposit-covered portion; the cash-COD portion has real, disclosed limits. |
| **Payment partner dependency** | PayMongo downtime or policy change directly halts the transaction flow — no current fallback. |
| **Growth pace assumption** | Word-of-mouth and personal outreach are strong first channels, but multi-week ramp time — not overnight — should be the planning assumption. |

*This document reflects the model as reasoned through to date. Figures marked as estimates (commission %, subscription pricing, cost ranges) are directional starting points meant to be pressure-tested against real seller and buyer behavior post-launch, not treated as final.*
